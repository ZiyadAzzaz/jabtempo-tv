JABTEMPO / START PLAYING

Your LEFT. Your RIGHT.

The laptop plays the game and music. Your iPhone, Android phone or tablet supplies the camera.

Back to cameraGame: open on laptop

1. Pair and start the phone camera

Choose Play on the TV. Enter its live code on your phone and tap Connect to TV. Then check camera consent, tap Start camera and allow access. Keep Safari/Chrome visible.

Example UI screenshots use synthetic test input; pairing codes and scores are examples, not your results.

2. Check both hands

Blue circle = your actual LEFT hand. Orange rounded square = your actual RIGHT. Follow TRY YOUR LEFT HAND, then TRY YOUR RIGHT HAND. Practice waits for the correct detected hand.

Keep face, both shoulders, elbows and wrists visible. Angle slightly so the camera sees the elbow straighten. Watch the phone's LEFT / RIGHT counters. If consistently swapped after checking your actual hand, confirm the mapping fix on the TV and repeat both hands.

3. Four LEFT, then four RIGHT

Choose Calibrate both hands, then Start calibration. WAIT 4, 3, 2, 1 means hold guard. Follow LEFT + RETURN until 4/4, then RIGHT + RETURN until 4/4.

Every other beat is OK: punch, return/rest, punch, return/rest. You have 24 opportunities. When the offset appears, select Start round.

4. Follow your two lanes

Notes move down. Extend the requested hand when the center reaches the white line; return to your chin. The silhouette highlights your next hand. Other hand! means use the opposite hand.

Settings offers Left/Right, Jab/Cross or Icons only. Stance changes boxing words; physical lanes stay LEFT and RIGHT. Solo Harder/Easier changes live tempo without resetting score. Pause/Resume is available on both devices.

5. Buttons, optional voice, family

The phone has seven command buttons. Enable local speech optionally loads an English model on your device. Hold to speak, say one command, then release. No audio goes to the TV or a cloud recognition service. Use buttons if speech fails.

Family Challenge: choose two names on the TV, take turns with the same chart/pace, then compare named scores. Real-time two-player tracking is future work.
